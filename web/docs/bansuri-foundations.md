Primary collection: [Divine Bansuri beginner playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWp4JCKvWekoWGmBRbE8Lx3hkG2uv0liF). The review is of captions, not listening to the audio or watching the demonstrations. Automatic captions frequently corrupt swar names and musical patterns. Descriptions such as “this finger,” silent demonstrations, lip position and the actual sound of an ornament cannot be recovered reliably from text. All timestamps below are caption anchors for subsequent video review, not verified frame-accurate transcriptions.

Distinguish three evidence classes throughout future work: **source teaching** (what this teacher says), **product proposal** (our interpretation/design), and **validated assessment** (requires recordings and instrument/teacher trials). A teacher's spoken instruction inside a source is reference content, not permission to operate the computer or change the app.

## The bansuri mindset

1. **Sound production is a recurring skill in its own right.** The beginner first finds a controlled tone without worrying about note names or covering holes. Clear tone remains part of every later exercise, including for experienced players returning to basics. The course begins this in lesson 2, before its theory lessons.
2. **The player continuously creates the sound.** Lip opening, direction of the air jet, the flute's angle, ongoing airflow and hole coverage interact. Guidance should describe air moving across the blowing-hole edge, rather than encourage forcefully blowing straight down a tube. General flute acoustics supports this explanation; its Western-flute fingering examples are not bansuri fingerings. [UNSW acoustics](https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/fluteacoustics.html).
3. **A named pitch and a well-controlled tone are separate achievements.** Correct Sa can be unstable, excessively airy, interrupted or in the wrong register. Conversely, a controlled sound may be on a different note. Do not collapse these into one green check or claim that a high detector confidence means beautiful tone.
4. **Fingering, air and register work together.** Closing a hole changes the sounding air column; leaks can disturb a note. The same basic hole pattern can produce more than one register. Alternate/vented fingerings also exist. A six-bit hole picture cannot by itself specify the whole playing action.
5. **Sa is a reference, not a universal C.** Once the player's middle Sa and convention are confirmed, describe swar and register relative to it, while retaining Western pitch/frequency as a useful secondary reference. A flute's key label alone does not establish the player's fingering, sounding register or tuning.
6. **Slow, controlled movement precedes speed.** Alankars train deliberate movement between swars; repeating a mechanically fast scale is not the whole goal. Add one difficulty at a time and repeat foundations rather than mark them permanently completed.
7. **Listening and musical expression matter.** Learners should hear the difference between a held tone, a break, a glide, a grace note and deliberate oscillation. Expressive movement that is correct in one drill would be instability in another. The evaluator must know the intended skill.
8. **Comfort, instrument and context affect results.** Hole spacing, hand reach, a flute's condition, room sound and microphone placement can change the experience. Sound alone does not identify a leaking finger, bad posture, lip position, poor instrument or lack of effort. Diagnose possibilities, not the player's body.

“Crystal clear” is the user's useful listening goal: controlled, consistent sound without unwanted hiss or breaks. It is not yet an operational acoustic score. Do not translate it into zero breath noise, maximum loudness, maximum breath duration, health claims or a universal spectral threshold. The source's breath-length/health statements are not adopted as app requirements.

## Conventions future code must preserve

- Primary notation for Hindustani bansuri: Sa/Re/Ga/Ma/Pa/Dha/Ni and explicit lower/middle/upper register; Western pitch remains secondary. Shuddha Ma and Tivra Ma must remain distinct. Text `m/M`, dots and apostrophes vary across sources; normalize internally and teach one visible legend.
- Use the established three-hole middle-Sa convention unless a separately supported instrument profile explicitly says otherwise. The course's lessons 4–5 shortcut is superseded by lesson 6 at about 01:45. Do not change the app's tonic definition to match that shortcut.
- For the six-hole profile, all-open corresponds to Tivra Ma; middle Sa closes holes 1–3 (counting from the blowing end), Re closes 1–2 and Ga closes 1. Six/five/four closures describe the low Pa/Dha/Ni branch. Higher-register playing can require alternate fingerings as well as air control. [Bansuri.com swars](https://bansuri.com/learn-bansuri/swaras-symbols/) corroborates the base convention, not every register-specific variant.
- Lesson 6's middle-Pa alternative opens hole 1 and closes 2–6. A simple “first N holes closed” representation cannot show it. Other teaching material also describes a vented middle/higher Pa; it is an alternative, not proof that all-six-closed overblowing is always invalid. [Bansuri Flute teaching guide](https://www.bansuriflute.co.uk/p/beginners-how-to-play-bansuri.html?m=0).
- Do not infer precise contact points, grip identity, handedness or blowing direction from a pitch. The owner explicitly wants **both hand orientations, default left hand nearest the blowing hole**. Label the choice by the near-hole hand, not dominant hand. Mirror visual hand placement without changing hole numbers, swar/register relationships or acoustic targets. This is an accepted requirement; the selector is not implemented by this research task.
- Purely acoustic checks remain incapable of proving the intended instrument or physical finger. Humming acceptance is a known source-recognition limitation, still outside the requested implementation scope.

## Teaching concepts versus microphone evidence

| Intended skill | Acoustic evidence worth exploring | What that evidence cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| First sound / embouchure exploration | Sound presence and detected periodic-tone intervals | Correct lip position, lack of excess hiss or actual bansuri source |
| Sustained tone | Pitch trajectory, longest continuous detected run, gaps and tuning drift | Breath support, comfort, physical cause of a gap or musical tone beauty |
| Hole changes | Ordered arrival at swars, register, continuity during a deliberate transition | Which fingers moved, whether a specific hole leaked, or exact contact pressure |
| Register control | Detected lower/upper arrival and accidental register jumps | Air speed/angle, posture or correct alternate fingering |
| Partial-hole tuning | Pitch against a declared reference, stable arrival at altered swar | A fixed geometric fraction of opening or universal raga intonation |
| Alankar/pulse | Ordered notes; later calibrated onset/subdivision evidence | Expressive phrasing from note correctness alone |
| Ornaments | Future pitch/envelope contours with a skill-specific expectation | Authentic/style-appropriate kan, meend, gamak, andolan, vibrato or tonguing from the current note gate |

## Current implementation gaps (source inspected at release ce96268)

1. `curriculum.ts` has `f1` “A steady Sa,” three one-second targets, then swar sequences. It has no exploratory first-sound drill or explicit tone-consistency workout per swar. `f0` is reflex practice, not the beginner foundation.
2. `NoteValidator` in `validation.ts` completes after the first successful hold. `usePractice.ts` then waits for release and advances; the remainder of the tone is not evaluated. All repeated/transition targets effectively need release. This cannot assess one continuous sustained tone or a connected two-note change.
3. `pitch.ts` exposes a YIN-style periodicity confidence, not a calibrated clarity/noise/technique score. The audio stream currently supplies RMS and pitch snapshots about every 60ms or later, with 2,048-sample analysis windows and a 100–2,000Hz supported detector range.
4. `visualModels.ts` maps pitch class modulo 12 to `{closed, half}`, losing register and alternate-hole information. The current diagram cannot show lesson 6's vented Pa, alternate Tivra-Ma–Pa transition, grip or orientation. Its half-filled symbol must not be interpreted as exactly 50% opening.
5. Full sargam already includes Shuddha Ma, while the app provides little partial-hole teaching. A prerequisite/explanation is needed; changing the label to Tivra Ma would change the exercise, not fix the instruction gap.
6. Existing records store note-attempt events and timing, not complete tone windows/trajectories or a versioned tone evaluator. Existing pitch-match rings cannot be relabeled as tone-quality progress.

Two further primary checks informed the proposal: [Bansuri.com first-sound instruction](https://bansuri.com/learn-bansuri/start-here/) also places all-open tone before sequential hole closure and supports both hand orientations. [W3C Media Capture and Streams](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/) distinguishes requested constraints, reported capabilities and current settings; disabled processing cannot simply be assumed from the request. These references support specific design choices, not certification of our app or a single universal teaching method.
